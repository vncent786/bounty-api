## Method that Bounty should implement

### 1. Detect a specific observation, not a broad theme

The book frames an observation as the start of an investigation, not the conclusion. A worthwhile hypothesis must identify a change capable of materially affecting sales, revenue, or profit and must not already be accepted by Wall Street (PDF p. 104, printed p. 83).

Product implication:

- `AI`, `fitness`, `inflation`, and raw post titles are not leads.
- A lead names the specific product, behavior, direction, mechanism, and affected economic exposure.

### 2. Replicate the observation and seek the reason

Camillo replicated a Coach-store observation across stores and states, compared it with nearby competitors, and used web chatter plus other investors’ store checks (PDF p. 106, printed p. 86). Follow-up interviews then showed that the visible lines reflected lower-ticket trade-down, not the bullish sales hypothesis. The idea was rejected despite a striking observation (PDF pp. 107–109).

Product implication:

- One viral post is an observation seed.
- Independent parent conversations, creators, communities, geographies, and corroborating channels determine breadth.
- The system must search for alternative explanations and be willing to reject a visually impressive signal.

### 3. Use conversational data before transactional confirmation

Camillo contrasts conversational data with the credit-card and other transactional data bought by institutions (video 00:24:55–00:25:43). Consumers often discuss intent, trial, switching, rejection, or repeat behavior before a purchase appears in transaction data (00:25:45–00:26:34).

He says TikTok comments have been a major recent source of alpha because people express themselves there across many topics (00:22:21–00:22:51). This does not mean “top TikTok posts” are leads. Comments are useful when they contain concrete behavior and when propagation, independence, and context are measured.

### 4. Benchmark speech against historical norms

Camillo describes TickerTags as using a 10% randomized Twitter sample, roughly 1.5 million curated word combinations in a company/product taxonomy, and monitoring frequencies against historical norms (video 00:29:49–00:31:27).

Product implication:

- Build and version a product/behavior/entity taxonomy.
- Compare a topic only against comparable observations from the same platform, query scope, geography, and collection method.
- Detect abnormal frequency, velocity, acceleration, independent-author breadth, and expansion into new communities.
- Never combine unlike platform metrics into a universal score.

### 5. Adapt to where consumers actually speak

Camillo says Twitter once contained broad life and product conversation, but by the time of the recent interview it had become more news-, finance-, and tech-oriented; broader consumer expression had migrated to platforms such as TikTok (video 00:31:46–00:32:22).

Product implication:

- X remains valuable for real-time news, specialist communities, contradiction, and information-parity checks.
- TikTok and Instagram are important for consumer discovery and comments.
- Platform roles must differ; five checkmarks are not the objective.

### 6. Test information parity, not merely media volume

The book uses opposing source lists and a consensus score to estimate whether the investment hypothesis is already accepted, then treats parity as the loss of the edge (PDF pp. 135–136). The exact numerical threshold is Camillo’s heuristic, not a universal statistic.

The recent video restates the requirement: determine whether the information is meaningful, a needle mover, genuinely off-radar, and not already accounted for by institutional or retail investors (00:03:34–00:04:12). In the e.l.f. example, the observation was followed by physical store verification and a direct check of analyst awareness (00:20:26–00:22:08).

Product implication:

- Separate truth from awareness. A true trend is not necessarily an edge.
- Check consumer media, specialist coverage, financial media, company disclosures, earnings calls, analyst commentary when available, and price reaction.
- Say what sources were checked. Never claim universal sell-side silence without licensed coverage.

### 7. Treat the crowd as discovery, not truth

The book explicitly says to use crowds to discover and challenge ideas, not accept their claims as true (PDF p. 161, printed p. 141). It requires independent verification of anonymous online claims (PDF p. 164, printed p. 144).

Product implication:

- Grok may classify, label, summarize, find contradictions, or suggest searches.
- Grok must not create the evidence corpus or decide that examples represent an anomaly without measured denominators and history.
- Every investor-facing claim must resolve to stored evidence.

### 8. Comprehensive coverage matters more than the loudest segment

In the Restaurant Brands example, Camillo monitored strong Popeyes and Burger King observations but missed severe Tim Hortons weakness, which invalidated the combined-company trade (video 00:55:00–00:58:27).

Product implication:

- After a topic qualifies, map the full company exposure before presenting likely beneficiaries or losers.
- Include all material segments, geographies, channels, and offsetting effects.
- Discovery is not restricted to options-enabled securities. Options, liquidity, borrow, and implementability are downstream attributes.

## Day-one versus forward monitoring

### What can work on day one

Day-one discovery is possible when a provider supplies enough timestamped historical posts/comments to construct comparable prior windows. Bounty can retrospectively identify:

- recent topic onset;
- anomaly versus historical norm;
- concentration in one viral parent versus independent spread;
- unique-author and community breadth;
- preliminary persistence within the backfilled window;
- specific behavior and reasons;
- present information-parity state.

A day-one label should say **Retrospective anomaly** and disclose the backfill range and source coverage.

### What still needs forward monitoring

Forward collection is required to establish:

- whether the signal persists, accelerates, or reverses after detection;
- whether it spreads into new cohorts and platforms;
- whether consumer intent converts into trial, repeat use, switching, or abandonment;
- whether parity changes and the edge closes;
- whether Bounty’s thresholds work prospectively without hindsight;
- actual provider reliability, gaps, and latency.

Therefore Bounty does not need a blank three-week onboarding period. It needs historical backfill for day-one candidates plus continuous monitoring for confidence updates and prospective validation.

## Required investor-facing lead

A promoted lead must show:

1. Specific behavior shift.
2. Why now versus its own historical baseline.
3. Independent breadth and propagation concentration.
4. Representative comments and source links.
5. Contradictions and alternative explanations.
6. Information-parity level and exactly which sources were checked.
7. Preliminary economic mechanism and full-exposure gaps.
8. Invalidation condition.
9. Status: retrospective anomaly, forward-confirming, confirmed, fading, or rejected.

If these fields cannot be supported, the correct output is **No qualified leads**, not raw social titles.
